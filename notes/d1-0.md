## Why this matters

HCF and LCM questions appear in nearly every aptitude test. They also hide inside other topics, such as bells ringing together, traffic lights, tiles and time and work. Two or three rules solve most of them in under a minute.

## Core concepts

### Definitions

- **HCF** (Highest Common Factor, also called GCD) is the largest number that divides all the given numbers.
- **LCM** (Least Common Multiple) is the smallest number that all the given numbers divide.

### Prime factorisation method

Write each number in prime form.

- HCF = product of common primes with the **lowest** powers.
- LCM = product of all primes with the **highest** powers.

For 72 = 2^3 × 3^2 and 120 = 2^3 × 3 × 5:

- HCF = 2^3 × 3 = 24
- LCM = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5 = 360

### Division method for HCF

Divide the larger number by the smaller, then divide the divisor by the remainder. Repeat until the remainder is 0. The last divisor is the HCF. This is Euclid's algorithm, and you will code it in Python later.

### The key formula for two numbers

HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.

This works only for two numbers, not three.

### Fractions

- HCF of fractions = HCF of numerators ÷ LCM of denominators.
- LCM of fractions = LCM of numerators ÷ HCF of denominators.

### Remainder patterns

| Question says | Answer is |
| --- | --- |
| Largest number that divides a, b, c exactly | HCF(a, b, c) |
| Largest number that divides a, b, c leaving the same remainder r | HCF(a - r, b - r, c - r) |
| Largest number that divides a, b, c leaving the same remainder, r unknown | HCF of the differences (b - a), (c - b) |
| Smallest number divisible by a, b, c | LCM(a, b, c) |
| Smallest number that leaves remainder r when divided by a, b, c | LCM(a, b, c) + r |
| Smallest number that leaves remainders a - k, b - k, c - k | LCM(a, b, c) - k |

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Using the formula

**The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 360. One number is 72. Find the other.**

Other number = 12 × 360 ÷ 72 = 60.

### Example 2: Bells ringing together

**Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes. They ring together at 9:00 AM. When will they next ring together?**

LCM(6, 8, 12) = 24. They ring together again at 9:24 AM.

### Example 3: Largest tile

**A room is 6 m 24 cm long and 4 m 32 cm wide. Find the largest square tile that fits exactly.**

Convert to cm: 624 and 432. HCF(624, 432) = 48. The tile side is 48 cm.

### Example 4: Same remainder

**Find the largest number that divides 43, 91 and 183 leaving the same remainder.**

Differences: 91 - 43 = 48 and 183 - 91 = 92. HCF(48, 92) = 4. Answer: 4.

### Example 5: Remainder pattern

**Find the smallest number that leaves remainder 3 when divided by 6, 9 and 15.**

LCM(6, 9, 15) = 90. Answer: 90 + 3 = 93.

### Example 6: Ratio with HCF

**Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5 and their LCM is 225. Find the numbers.**

Let them be 3x and 5x. LCM = 15x = 225, so x = 15. The numbers are 45 and 75.

## Common mistakes

- Using HCF × LCM = product for three numbers. It only works for two.
- Mixing units, such as metres and centimetres, before finding the HCF.
- Using the lowest powers for LCM. LCM always takes the highest powers.
- Forgetting that the HCF always divides the LCM.

## Interview and test questions

**Can the HCF of two numbers be larger than their LCM?**
No. The HCF always divides the LCM, so it is never larger.

**What is the HCF of two co-prime numbers?**
1, and their LCM is their product.

**What is HCF × LCM for 12 and 18?**
6 × 36 = 216, which equals 12 × 18.

**How do you find the HCF of 0.6 and 0.84?**
Write them as 60 and 84 hundredths. HCF(60, 84) = 12, so the answer is 0.12.

**Which algorithm finds the GCD quickly in code?**
Euclid's algorithm: gcd(a, b) = gcd(b, a % b) until b is 0.

## Quick recap

- HCF takes common primes with the lowest powers, and LCM takes all primes with the highest powers.
- HCF × LCM = product, for two numbers only.
- "Largest that divides" means HCF, and "smallest divisible by" means LCM.
- Same unknown remainder: take the HCF of the differences.
- Always convert to the same units first.
