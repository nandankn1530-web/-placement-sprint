## Why this matters

Number system questions appear in almost every placement test, including TCS NQT, Infosys and Wipro. They are quick to solve once you know a few rules, so they are easy marks. Many harder topics, such as HCF, LCM and remainders, are built on these basics.

## Core concepts

### Types of numbers

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural numbers | Counting numbers | 1, 2, 3, ... |
| Whole numbers | Natural numbers plus zero | 0, 1, 2, ... |
| Integers | Whole numbers and their negatives | -3, 0, 7 |
| Prime numbers | Exactly two factors: 1 and itself | 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 |
| Composite numbers | More than two factors | 4, 6, 9, 12 |
| Co-primes | Two numbers whose HCF is 1 | 8 and 15 |

Remember that 1 is neither prime nor composite, and 2 is the only even prime.

### Divisibility rules

| Divisible by | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Last digit is even | 3,514 |
| 3 | Sum of digits divisible by 3 | 471: 4+7+1 = 12 |
| 4 | Last two digits divisible by 4 | 1,316: 16 |
| 5 | Last digit 0 or 5 | 2,345 |
| 6 | Divisible by both 2 and 3 | 612 |
| 8 | Last three digits divisible by 8 | 5,120: 120 |
| 9 | Sum of digits divisible by 9 | 7,281: 18 |
| 11 | (Sum of odd-place digits) minus (sum of even-place digits) is 0 or a multiple of 11 | 2,728: (2+2) - (7+8) = -11 |

### Remainders

When a number N is divided by d, we write N = d × q + r, where 0 ≤ r < d.

Two rules save a lot of time:

- The remainder of a sum equals the remainder of the sum of remainders.
- The remainder of a product equals the remainder of the product of remainders.

### Unit digit

The unit digit of a power repeats in a cycle.

| Last digit | Cycle | Cycle length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, 1, 5, 6 | Always itself | 1 |
| 4 | 4, 6 | 2 |
| 9 | 9, 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 2, 4, 8, 6 | 4 |
| 3 | 3, 9, 7, 1 | 4 |
| 7 | 7, 9, 3, 1 | 4 |
| 8 | 8, 4, 2, 6 | 4 |

To find the unit digit of a^n, divide n by 4 and use the remainder. If the remainder is 0, use the 4th value of the cycle.

### Number of factors

If N = a^p × b^q × c^r in prime form, then the number of factors is (p+1)(q+1)(r+1).

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Divisibility

**Is 91,476 divisible by 12?**

A number divisible by 12 must be divisible by 3 and 4. The digit sum is 9+1+4+7+6 = 27, so it is divisible by 3. The last two digits, 76, are divisible by 4. So yes, it is divisible by 12.

### Example 2: Missing digit

**Find the smallest digit x so that 5x72 is divisible by 9.**

Digit sum = 5 + x + 7 + 2 = 14 + x. The next multiple of 9 is 18, so x = 4.

### Example 3: Remainder of a product

**Find the remainder when 47 × 53 is divided by 5.**

47 leaves 2 and 53 leaves 3. The product of remainders is 6, which leaves 1. Answer: 1.

### Example 4: Remainder of a big power

**Find the remainder when 2^100 is divided by 3.**

2 leaves remainder -1 when divided by 3. So 2^100 leaves (-1)^100 = 1. Answer: 1.

### Example 5: Unit digit

**Find the unit digit of 7^95.**

95 divided by 4 leaves 3. The third value in the cycle of 7 is 3. Answer: 3.

### Example 6: Number of factors

**How many factors does 360 have?**

360 = 2^3 × 3^2 × 5^1. Factors = (3+1)(2+1)(1+1) = 24.

## Common mistakes

- Treating 1 as a prime number.
- Using the rule for 8 on the last two digits instead of the last three.
- Forgetting that a remainder of 0 in the unit digit cycle means the last value of the cycle.
- Adding remainders but forgetting to take the remainder again at the end.

## Interview and test questions

**What is the only even prime number?**
2.

**When is a number divisible by 11?**
When the difference between the sums of digits at odd and even places is 0 or a multiple of 11.

**What is the unit digit of 4 raised to an odd power?**
4. For an even power it is 6.

**How do you count the factors of a number quickly?**
Write it in prime form, add 1 to each power and multiply.

**What are co-prime numbers?**
Two numbers whose only common factor is 1, such as 9 and 16.

## Quick recap

- 1 is neither prime nor composite, and 2 is the only even prime.
- Learn divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11.
- Remainders can be added and multiplied, then reduced again.
- Unit digits repeat in cycles of 1, 2 or 4.
- Number of factors equals the product of each prime power plus one.
